# Assignment Brief

A prompt that works once is a fluke. A prompt that works every time, for anyone on the team, in any tool, is a brief. Fill in all five parts before you paste anything into an AI tool.

Never skip a part. If a part is genuinely empty, that is the part your output will drift on.

## The five parts

### 1. The backstory

What does it need to know, and what must not happen?

- Who you are and what role the AI is thinking as
- What has already been decided
- The source material you are handing over
- One line: "Do not add anything the source does not say."

### 2. The job

Who reads this, and what does it cost if it is wrong?

- The task: clarify, decide, persuade, document, or summarize
- The reader, named specifically
- The cost of a mistake, stated concretely. "If a date is wrong, someone misses a deadline" works. "Accuracy is important" does not.

### 3. The rules

Tone, length, format, and one never.

- Tone and voice
- Length as a number
- Format and structure
- At least one thing it must never do

### 4. How to think

How should it reason, and what should it flag?

- Evaluate before responding
- Flag gaps instead of filling them
- Be honest, not agreeable
- Argue against the draft where that serves the reader

### 5. The check

What do you look at before you trust it?

- Every name, date, number, quote, and citation gets verified
- Re-read the output against the original job
- Cut what does not serve the reader

Anything the output claims is unverified until a human checks the source.

## Template

```
THE BACKSTORY
You are helping <who> as <role>. We have already decided <decisions>. The source is <material>. Do not add anything the source does not say.

THE JOB
<Clarify | decide | persuade | document | summarize> for <named reader>. If this is wrong, <concrete cost>.

THE RULES
Tone: <tone>. Length: <number>. Format: <structure>. Never <one thing>.

HOW TO THINK
Evaluate before you respond. Flag anything the source does not say instead of filling it in. Be honest with me rather than agreeable.

THE CHECK
Before you finish, list every name, date, number, and quote you used and where it came from. Cut anything that does not serve the reader.
```

Then add two lines for whoever runs it:

- **Run it on:** what to paste alongside the brief
- **Watch for:** the most likely way this particular task goes wrong

## The weak version, for contrast

```
Summarize these meeting notes.
```

Same task. No reader, no cost, no rules, no check. The output will be confident, generic, and wrong in a way nobody catches until it matters. The gap between the two versions is the whole lesson.
